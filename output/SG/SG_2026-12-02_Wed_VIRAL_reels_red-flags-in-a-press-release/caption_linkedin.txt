Five red flags that make editors stop reading a press release.
All of them are easy to fix before you send.

None of these are about writing talent. They are process gaps that a two-minute review would catch.

1. A headline with no verb: if nothing happens, there is no news.
2. The news buried below the fold: lead with it.
3. Unverified superlatives such as "world's first": journalists check, and credibility drops when they can't.
4. Long pitches: Muck Rack (2025) found 69% of journalists prefer pitches under 200 words.
5. A generic contact: name a person who will answer the phone today.
