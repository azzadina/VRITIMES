72% of journalists say press releases are the most useful resource PR teams provide.
The format isn't the problem. Most releases just can't be used as they are.

Cision's 2025 State of the Media report (3,000+ journalists, 19 countries) is often quoted as proof that "releases still work". The part that gets skipped: journalists use the releases that save them work, not the ones that create more.
For Australian teams, the releases that end up published tend to share the same anatomy:

1. The headline is news: subject + action + impact, 10–14 words, ideally with a number or a location.
2. The lead answers 5W1H in two sentences. If it doesn't, the editor stops there.
3. The quote carries a point of view or data, not a line about being thrilled.
4. The body uses an inverted pyramid with comparative numbers, high-res photos and a contact who responds.
5. A three-sentence boilerplate refreshed every quarter, because journalists copy it verbatim.
