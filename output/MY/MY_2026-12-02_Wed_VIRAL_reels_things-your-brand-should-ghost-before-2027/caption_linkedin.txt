Five PR habits worth retiring before 2027.
A light video, a serious point.

Most of these survive because nobody questions them in the planning meeting. A quick audit:

1. Superlatives in headlines. Editors look for a number, a place or an impact, not "leading".
2. Empty quotes. If it couldn't appear in an article, cut it.
3. Daily follow-ups. Muck Rack (2025) found 50% of journalists say one follow-up is ideal, and 51% want it 3–7 days later.
4. AVE reports. AMEC's Barcelona Principles 4.0 (2025) say invalid measures such as AVEs should not be used. Report outcomes and business impact instead.
5. Waiting for a journalist to stumble on your social post instead of sending news to the right desk.
