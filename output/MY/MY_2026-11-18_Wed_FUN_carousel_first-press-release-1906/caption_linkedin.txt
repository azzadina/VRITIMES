The release often called the first ever didn't announce a product. It came out of a crisis.
The lessons still apply to businesses today.

In October 1906, a train run by a Pennsylvania Railroad subsidiary came off a bridge in Atlantic City. The industry norm was to restrict access and say nothing.
Ivy Lee did the opposite. He drafted an official statement, shared it with reporters and gave them access to the site. The New York Times ran it word for word on 30 October 1906.
Three principles from that day we still see working:

1. Whoever speaks first with facts frames the story.
2. A statement that can be quoted as is gets used more than a claim.
3. Access and convenience for journalists are part of the strategy, not a bonus.
